Claude Fable 5.2 vs Gemini 4 Carbon: Intelligence, Cost and Real-World Trade-offs

By DekrovPublished November 11, 2026Updated November 11, 2026

A matched-effort head-to-head of the two frontier models: independent benchmark scores, API pricing, product access, real developer concerns and why there is no universal winner.

Start with the plain-English explanation ↓

Overview

Anthropic released Claude Fable 5.2 on November 9, 2026. Google announced Gemini 4 Carbon in October, then opened its public paid API and began its AI Ultra product rollout on November 10. This creates a real head-to-head between two frontier models aimed at complex coding, scientific research and long-running agents—but access, price and safety policy differ. [1] [2] [4]

The answer changes with thinking effort. In independent Artificial Analysis comparisons, Fable 5.2 scores 62 versus Carbon's 60 at high effort. At medium, Carbon scores 58 and Fable 56. Fable's max score rises to 65 against Carbon high at 60, but the associated benchmark spend and time grow substantially. There is no defensible one-number winner without specifying the tested workload and effort settings. [6] [7] [8]

This report is a new comparison that incorporates post-launch independent tests and new Carbon public access, not a duplicate announcement of Fable or Carbon. It separates observed scores from vendor marketing and developer opinion. Dekrov did not run a paid controlled API benchmark.

  2. Nov 9 Anthropic releases Fable 5.2 and the access-restricted Mythos sibling. [4]

Technical Deep Dive

Reasoning effort is a test condition, not a footnote

Fable 5.2 offers multiple inference-effort levels, and Carbon has its own distinct settings. Vendor marketing often presents the strongest result for each model, but those runs may not use comparable time or compute budgets. Anthropic's FrontierCode 2.0 row compares Fable at xhigh and Carbon at medium, which does not mean Fable is always better at an equal budget. The independent high/high and medium/medium comparisons below let developers see the reversal rather than repeating a mixed-effort claim. [4] [6] [8]

What each vendor reports, and how it differs from external tests

Anthropic's November 9 release reports Fable 5.2 at 69.9% on FrontierSWE v2 versus Carbon at 61.2%, while its business AutomationBench table gives Carbon 53.9% versus Fable 51.4%. Anthropic also reports 49.1% on FrontierCode 2.0 at xhigh for Fable against Carbon's 41.1% at medium. Google's Carbon model card contains a separate technical table and methodological notes. These are traceable vendor-reported results—but neither table proves that all listed scores were obtained with identical tool settings, token allowances or safety policies. [3] [4]

Google's November 10 update separately names wins on CursorBench 4.0, Perplexity WANDR and Zapier's AutomationBench, while Anthropic advertises applications ranging from software migrations to robotics and science. Those use cases are interesting but not substitute evidence for a team's actual maintenance tasks. [1] [4]

Context length and modality are not equivalent to intelligence

Both models offer a one-million-token context window. Google's Carbon card permits text, images, audio and video inputs and describes a combined input/output token budget; Fable's public documentation lists text and images as inputs, text output, and a 128K maximum output. Long-context capacity does not prove consistent recall or reasoning quality at the limit. A multimodal product requiring video should account for supported inputs before comparing the text reasoning scores. [3] [5]

The critical November pricing caveat

As of November 11, Google's public Carbon API price is a temporary $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens. The company says pricing changes after November 30, 2026 to $4/$20. Anthropic lists Fable 5.2 at $8/$40 without that promotion. Thus Fable's list per-token cost is four times Carbon's current introductory rate, then twice the listed post-promotion rate, before caching, output length or volume discounts. Some independent model-comparison tables use the future $4/$20 Carbon price—do not present it as the current launch price. [1] [5] [6]

This higher per-token price does not imply that total task time will fall by that factor: reasoning, tool calls and workflow overhead remain. The Fable platform documentation also publishes caching and batch rates; any real invoice depends on the actual chosen tier and usage pattern. [4] [5]

Availability: a crucial correction to older comparisons

Google's October 28 announcement was initially restricted to select cybersecurity defenders. On November 10 Google explicitly expanded Carbon to paying API developers and enterprise users and began a Gemini/Antigravity/AI Mode rollout for Google AI Ultra subscribers. Its specialized Fairwind cybersecurity access remains a separate program. A November 10 secondary comparison that says Carbon is still Fairwind-only has therefore become outdated; the vendor's newer release is the better source for present access. This does not mean all free or AI Pro accounts have Carbon today. [1] [2] [11]

Anthropic lists Fable 5.2 as active on the Claude API and supported enterprise cloud routes. Anthropic's Mythos 5.2 shares an underlying model with Fable but has different cyber/biology safeguards and is restricted to verified organizations. Conflating Mythos cyber scores with ordinary public Fable access would distort this comparison. Fable's API also has specific restrictions: adaptive thinking stays on, certain forced tool-choice settings return errors, and some sampling fields should be omitted. Review the latest integration documentation before migration. [4] [5]

Cybersecurity scores need different treatment

Google's Carbon announcement reports 88.2% on SEC-bench Pro, 54.8% on ExploitGym and 71.0% on CWE-bench, under particular evaluator conditions. Anthropic's detailed security benchmarks describe the restricted Mythos variant and different benchmark suites. This report has no verified head-to-head on the same cyber benchmark, policy and environment. Claiming 'Fable loses cybersecurity' or 'Carbon wins all security work' from these incomparable reports would be unsound. Organizations need to assess permissions, guardrails and their own defensive workloads. [2] [3] [4]

Evidence, Independent Tests and Developer Reactions

Independent testing is more useful—but still limited

Artificial Analysis reports effort-labeled scores along with estimated cost, token usage and task time. That permits real trade-off discussions instead of treating a single launch score as a final ranking. Yet outcomes remain benchmark-specific, with unknown transfer to individual codebases and possible routing or evaluator changes. A 62 versus 60 difference does not imply statistical certainty for every domain. The numbers here are a dated source snapshot, and Dekrov has not run these models directly for this comparison. [6] [7] [8]

What developers are discussing

In the Fable launch Hacker News thread, some developers praise its difficult reasoning, code generation and visual JavaScript output; others report limited improvement over cheaper Opus 5.5 for ordinary programming. Some criticize the mixed-effort headline comparison and highlight visible errors in example websites. These are individual anecdotes or selectively shared tests, not controlled community consensus. [9]

In a Carbon availability thread, developers discuss Antigravity usage and ask each other whether they have tried Fable 5.2. One participant says they were impressed by Fable but had not yet tested Carbon. Such comments indicate what practitioners care about—latency, product access and agent harness—not a verified winner. Older Reddit speculation about unreleased Carbon or Fable 5.1 usage limits should not be treated as direct Fable 5.2 versus Carbon evidence. [10]

A controlled comparison a team could perform

  1. Collect representative production tasks, including multi-file fixes, code review, long-document reasoning, complex browser automation and repeatable terminal workflows.
  2. Pin prompt and repository state. Test both models under the same tools and quality criteria, but report separate time-budget and money-budget comparisons rather than assuming different effort names mean identical compute.
  3. Repeat noisy tasks, record individual failures and test each model's medium and high modes. Use Fable max only when evaluating whether its extra spend produces more completed tasks.
  4. Record response quality, all tool calls, output and reasoning tokens, API rate, caching, retries, elapsed time and actual billed spend. Independently review security- or finance-critical changes.
  5. Publish unsuccessful examples and exact environment settings. A high output speed can still coexist with slow loops or expensive verification.

Those are proposed methods, not experiments actually performed in this report.

Explained Simply

Two very smart specialists

Imagine hiring two expert contractors. Fable is especially good at certain difficult questions and complex coding work. Carbon can be better for some practical terminal and workflow tasks, and Google charges less per token during the current promotion. If both are asked to fix the same difficult software issue, the best worker depends on the job.

Why the winner changes with how hard they think

Both specialists can spend different amounts of time planning. At low or medium effort, one may get a better result at a lower cost. If you let the other spend much more time checking its thinking, it might solve more problems—but you pay for that time. AI benchmark 'effort settings' describe that trade-off, so it is unfair to compare only the highest number on a poster.

What is a benchmark?

A benchmark is a collection of test jobs, such as fixing bugs, reading data and completing software tasks. A score tells you how often a model succeeded on those jobs. Different tests can reward different strengths. It is possible for Fable to lead an overall reasoning index while Carbon wins a practical terminal test, with neither result being wrong.

A cheap unit price does not always mean a cheap project

Imagine one expert charges half as much per page but writes twice as many pages and takes longer. The total bill could still be comparable. AI models charge for tokens, which are small pieces of text and reasoning. That's why this report separates API cost per token from the estimated total cost and time to finish benchmark tasks.

Can you actually use them today?

Fable 5.2 can be accessed through Anthropic's supported services and APIs. Carbon was initially gated but Google broadened paid access on November 10, with an AI Ultra consumer rollout. Availability depends on your plan and product; a free user or AI Pro subscriber should not assume Carbon is unlocked. High-risk cybersecurity variants have additional restrictions. [1] [4] [5]

Which should you choose?

Try Fable first if you prioritize hard, open-ended research or intricate coding and can pay more. Try Carbon first if you need strong automation, audio/video input, or lower per-token costs. For important work, give them both one of your real projects with the same budget, then check the finished result—not only the first answer.

Conclusion

The most defensible verdict is conditional. At matched high reasoning effort, Fable 5.2 leads the independent aggregate and several coding/reasoning tests. Carbon leads some terminal and automation tasks and is substantially cheaper per token during its introductory public API period. At medium effort, Carbon leads the aggregate while Fable is faster and cheaper per benchmark task in the cited evaluation. At Fable max, intelligence improves but the measured cost grows sharply. [1] [6] [7] [8]

The fresh November 10 Carbon public rollout also invalidates older Fairwind-only comparisons. Keep access, benchmark effort and billing date visible in every model recommendation. A real workflow trial, not a marketing infographic, should decide a team's final choice. Previously published Carbon launch background is retained for history; this page covers the new Fable head-to-head. [1] [11]
